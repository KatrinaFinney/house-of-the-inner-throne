## Begin with the Atmosphere

Choose how you want to enter the practice.

### Cultural Ground

Incense has many religious histories. African American Hoodoo includes incense and fumigation within a larger world of prayer, cleansing, and spiritual supplies. Using incense does not make a practice Hoodoo by itself.

The materials below have documented practitioner associations. The brief routines are Shrine adaptations. A practice without smoke can be equally meaningful; shared air, house rules, and personal needs help determine the method.

## Material and Use

Learn a few materials before collecting many.

### A Small Incense Reference - Sources 1-4

- Frankincense | Use: prayer, spiritual devotion, strengthening a working. It can accompany a focused devotional sitting.
- Myrrh | Use: devotional incense and blends. The source documents its Biblical associations; it need not be assigned a universal purpose.
- Benzoin | Use: purification, luck, peace of mind. Consider it when studying traditional household-cleansing incense.
- Pine | Use: cleansing, steady prosperity. The association belongs to identified pine material, not every evergreen or pine-scented product.

### Choose a Usable Form

A clearly labeled incense product with instructions is simpler for beginners than improvising with raw resins. Sticks and cones still produce smoke. Resins may require specialized equipment; this guide does not teach indoor charcoal burning.

A product named 'prosperity' or 'protection' may have undisclosed ingredients. The name alone does not identify its composition. Avoid burning unknown powders, essential oils, treated wood, craft materials, or grocery herbs merely because they smell pleasant.

## Prepare the Space

Clean first. Then decide whether fragrance belongs.

### A Short Setup

- Ask anyone sharing the space whether smoke or fragrance is welcome.
- Choose a smokeless practice when anyone present is sensitive or when pets, housing restrictions, or ventilation make smoke unsuitable.
- If burning a suitable product, use the holder and surface its instructions require, clear nearby combustible materials, and supervise it throughout.
- Keep the practice brief, ventilate, and extinguish fully. Do not move a hot burner or handle hot ash.
- Charcoal produces carbon monoxide as well as particles; it is not the beginner indoor option here. Stop if smoke causes discomfort.

### Source of the Safety Guidance

EPA identifies candles and incense as indoor particle sources and notes that combustion can release carbon monoxide (Source 5). Ventilation does not make every product or exposure suitable for every household.

## Three Ways to Begin

Original Shrine practices, with smoke optional.

### A Prayer Sitting

1. Clear a small surface and write the purpose of your prayer.
2. Choose silence, a bell, or a suitable incense product. If burning, follow its instructions and stay present.
3. Read the prayer slowly. Sit comfortably for a few minutes.
4. Complete the session, extinguish if needed, and note your next action.

> May this space make room for clear thought, sincere prayer, and peaceful attention.

### A Household Welcome

- Remove obstacles from the doorway and clean the entrance.
- Speak what you welcome: rest, mutual care, fair exchange, and privacy.
- Use one gentle bell sound or a spoken blessing to mark the threshold. This version needs no smoke.
- Choose one household action that makes the welcome real.

### A Work Session

- Open your project and name one task you can complete.
- Use an unlit incense stick in a closed container as a visual reminder, or choose no fragrance at all.
- Begin the task. Close the session with a note of what you finished.

## Keep What Serves

The amount of smoke is not the measure of the practice.

### Smokeless Alternatives

Try deliberate silence, song, a bell, a written prayer, changed lighting, or a small bowl of plain water. Choose what fits your space and beliefs. Do not use fragrance to cover a gas smell, mold, or another problem that needs repair.

- Was the method welcome to everyone sharing the space?
- What would make the next session simpler?
