## Begin with One Light

A plain candle and a clear purpose are enough.

### Cultural Ground

Candle work is part of many devotional and magical traditions, including African American Hoodoo. Prayer, petitions, and candle selection can work together. Methods differ between workers; this guide offers an accessible introduction.

Color uses below draw from contemporary Hoodoo practitioner sources. The short rituals are original Shrine adaptations. A candle supports spiritual practice without guaranteeing an outcome or taking another person's choice away.

### Choose a Candle

- Tea light: a compact choice for a short sitting, in its proper holder.
- Taper or small altar candle: useful for a focused prayer or repeated, supervised sessions.
- Glass-encased candle: a devotional format that still needs supervision and an undamaged container.
- Unlit candle or battery light: an alternative where open flame is unsuitable. Burning longer is not a measure of spiritual seriousness.

## Color and Use

Choose the color that fits the work; there is no need to buy them all.

### A Practical Color Reference - Sources 1-4

- White | Use: prayer, blessing, spiritual clarity. Example: light a plain white candle before a prayer for clear direction.
- Green | Use: money drawing, prosperity, harvest. Example: pair it with a petition for fairly paid work.
- Pink | Use: romance, friendship, attraction. Example: pray for a loving relationship that is mutual and freely chosen.
- Red | Use: passionate love, sexual attraction. Example: honor consensual affection with a partner.
- Blue | Use: peace, household harmony, spiritual devotion. Example: name the quality of conversation you want at home.
- Orange | Use: opening roads, change. Example: pair a new-opportunity petition with a concrete application or introduction.
- Purple | Use: mastery, personal power, success. Example: pray for confidence while preparing to present your work.
- Yellow | Use: success, wealth, devotion. Example: pair a clear goal with a prayer and an action plan.
- Black | Use: repelling harmful influences, freedom from evil. Example: pray for release from a harmful pattern; the color alone does not signify harmful work.

### Make a Deliberate Choice

These meanings are not universal across religions or workers. If you use a different inherited system, name it and learn its context. Keep a record of the choice rather than searching endlessly for the perfect color.

## Prepare Simply

The most useful preparation is a clear, safe place to work.

### Before You Light

1. Write one sentence naming your purpose. Keep the petition beside the candle, outside the heat and flame area.
2. Set an undamaged candle in a proper, stable holder on a heat-resistant surface.
3. Keep at least 12 inches between the flame and anything that can burn. Clear curtains, paper, decorations, hair, and loose sleeves.
4. Trim and use the wick according to the manufacturer's directions. Leave the candle plain; our beginner practices need no oils or herbs on the wax.
5. Choose a session you can supervise. Keep children and animals away, and extinguish before leaving or sleeping.

### Repeat Sessions

If the candle is designed to be relit, you may return to the same prayer over several sessions. Extinguishing it safely does not cancel the work. Never commit to an uninterrupted burn that would require leaving it unattended.

## Five Ways to Begin

These brief practices are Shrine adaptations, not fixed traditional formulas.

### Choose One

- Protection: use a white candle for a household blessing. Name who and what you are protecting, then take one practical step to secure your space.
- Prosperity: use green. Write the kind of work or fair exchange you seek; follow with a relevant application, offer, or financial decision.
- Love: use pink. Ask for affection that is mutual and welcome. Pair the prayer with honest communication or a caring act.
- Peace: use blue. Name the tone you want in your home. Afterward, make one change that supports it.
- Clarity: use white. Write your question and three known facts. Sit quietly, then name your next step.

### A Short Session

1. Read the petition once and light the candle safely.
2. Pray, speak, or sit quietly for a few minutes. Keep your attention on the purpose rather than demanding a sign.
3. Extinguish safely, record one insight, and take your chosen action.

> May this light accompany clear prayer, steady courage, and responsible action.

## Carry the Work Forward

The prayer continues through what you do next.

### Reading a Burn

Wax, soot, and flame behavior are interpreted in some rootwork traditions. Physical causes such as wick condition and drafts also matter. Stop an unsafe burn; no interpretation is worth a fire. An unusual flame is not proof that someone is working against you.

### Reflect

- What did I ask for?
- What action followed the prayer?
- What happened, and what remains uncertain?
