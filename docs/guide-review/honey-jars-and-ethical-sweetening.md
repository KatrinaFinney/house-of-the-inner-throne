## Begin with Sweetness

Choose the condition you want to nurture.

### Cultural Ground

Sweetening work with sugar, honey, and other sweeteners has a documented place in African American Hoodoo. Containers and methods vary; a honey jar is one form rather than the whole tradition (Source 1).

The Shrine's examples focus on household kindness, business goodwill, and freely chosen love. This is our ethical approach; historical sweetening work is broader. These exercises do not promise to change someone's feelings or override a refusal.

### Three Useful Purposes

- Household peace: more patient speech and shared consideration.
- Business goodwill: welcome, fair exchange, and a good experience for the people your work serves.
- Reciprocal love: warmth and affection that everyone involved is free to choose.

## Choose Your Materials

A small, simple jar is enough.

### Material and Use

- Honey | Use: a sweetening medium. Choose a small amount; expensive honey is unnecessary.
- Sugar | Use: an alternative sweetening medium when honey is unavailable or unsuitable.
- Plain paper | Use: hold the names or condition in a short petition.
- Clean, dry jar and secure lid | Use: contain the working. A reused food jar is fine if sound and thoroughly cleaned.
- Label | Use: distinguish the ritual jar from food and record its date and purpose.

### Keep It Simple

Choose one sweetener, not a long shopping list. Our starter version uses no water, fresh plants, oils, bodily materials, or candle on the lid. It is a ritual object, not something to eat. Keep it labeled and away from children, pets, heat, and pests.

### About Timing

Honey is often used as an image of patience, but it does not establish how quickly a result will arrive. Choose a manageable time to return to the prayer. A weekly rhythm here is a Shrine suggestion, not a universal Hoodoo rule.

## Write a Clear Petition

Ask for the quality of relationship you can support.

### Ready-to-Adapt Examples

- Home: 'May our household speak with patience, listen with respect, and make room for one another.'
- Work: 'May the right customers meet my work with goodwill, and may I serve them fairly and well.'
- Love: 'May affection in my life be mutual, honest, kind, and freely chosen.'
- Community: 'May our gathering welcome sincere connection and shared care.'

### Names and Privacy

A condition-focused petition can work without naming another person. If you include names, use them respectfully and keep the paper private. Do not collect anyone's intimate or bodily materials without consent.

## Make a Simple Sweetening Jar

This candle-free version is an original Shrine adaptation.

### The Practice

1. Clean and fully dry the jar and lid. Set out your paper, pen, and chosen sweetener.
2. Write one short petition. Add your name or the name of the household or project if useful.
3. Fold the paper to fit. You may fold toward yourself to symbolize welcoming; this is optional.
4. Place it in the jar and add enough honey or dry sugar to cover it. Close the lid securely.
5. Hold the closed jar while reading the petition. Name one action you will take to bring the same quality into daily life.
6. Label the jar 'Ritual use - do not eat,' with the date and purpose. Place it somewhere stable and private.

> May my words and actions make room for kindness. May goodwill be met with goodwill.

### Pair Prayer with Conduct

For a home jar, follow with a patient conversation. For a business jar, improve the customer experience. For love, respect the other person's pace and answer. You can revisit the petition without opening, shaking, or adding to the jar.

## Review and Close

A practice can end without being a failure.

### After a Few Weeks

Notice your own conduct and ordinary evidence of change. No response from another person is a reason to respect their boundary, not to escalate the working. Stop if the jar becomes a focus of anxiety or repeated checking.

When finished, thank the practice and dispose of the contents in ordinary household waste according to local guidance; avoid drains and outdoor dumping. Recycle the cleaned container if suitable. Do not open a damaged or pressurized jar near your face.

### Reflect

- What did I do to make the situation kinder?
- What changed, and what did not?
- What belongs to someone else's choice?
